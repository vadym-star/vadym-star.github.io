A third colour

You asked whether the design could carry a third colour alongside the green and the blue, so that the page stops reading as green throughout. Here are the three that are worth considering, each shown on the parts of the homepage it would actually touch.

Nothing here changes the buttons, the links or the headings. The colour is put on the small things only: the label above a section, the ticks beside a list, the years figure over the hero photograph, and the cross that opens a question. That is enough to change how the page reads without turning it into a different design.

The three

Ochre, plum, deep teal

Each of these is built to the same weight as the green already in use, the same brightness and the same strength of colour as the eye reads them, with only the hue turned. That is what makes one of them sit beside the green as a relation rather than as something borrowed from elsewhere.

Ochre

#C47300 · warm, 76° round from the green

The warm answer. It sits well against green, ties in with the amber of the stars on the reviews, and reads as paper and ink rather than as a technology brand.

The catch: it is an orange, and you have said the design is better for not being orange.

Plum

#834791 · 179° round from the green, almost opposite it

The furthest thing from both the green and the blue that is not warm. Green and plum is an old pairing, botanical and academic rather than technical. It is deliberately dark: a bright purple reads cheap, a deep plum reads considered.

The catch: it is the boldest of the three, and it is unmistakably a new colour rather than a shade of what is there.

Deep teal

#006768 · 57° from the green, 65° from the blue

The instrument colour: laboratory equipment, charts, dials. Of the three it is the one that speaks most directly to science and technology, and it is quiet enough to use anywhere on the page.

The catch: it sits between the green and the blue rather than away from them, so beside the blue band it can read as a third shade of the same family.

How to look at these

Ignore the swatches and look at the label above the sample and the small circles beside the list. Those are the two places the colour is repeated most often on a page, and they are where a wrong choice shows first.

Whichever you prefer, it goes on in an afternoon and can come off just as quickly, so there is nothing to lose by trying one on the real pages for a few days before deciding.
